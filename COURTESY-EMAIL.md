**Subject:** Data licence question on your JAMA Network Open data-extraction audit

Dear Dr Huang,

I read your systematic review on data-extraction errors in diagnostic meta-analyses with interest,
and I am grateful you deposited the trial-level dataset openly. It made independent work possible.

Two questions, both from someone building tooling rather than reporting results:

**1. Licence.** The OSF project (osf.io/pb3q2) does not appear to carry a licence tag, though your
data-sharing statement says the data are available for any purpose. If you are willing to attach a
CC-BY licence, it would remove ambiguity for anyone building on it — including us. I have not
redistributed the file and will not without written terms.

**2. A methods question.** In your supplement, the pooled estimates are reported per meta-analysis
under anonymised identifiers. Is there a mapping between those identifiers and the underlying
reviews or outcomes that you would be willing to share? Without one, an outside group cannot
validate a re-implementation against your results — nearest-value matching is not a substitute, as
I discovered the hard way, since with this many values it matches at random.

I am not reporting any error rate, and I am not claiming to have found anything in individual
reviews. My interest is in whether automated verification of this literature is feasible at all, and
your data is the best-positioned resource for answering that.

With thanks for the paper,
